# Back this up

The `/data` volume holds three things you cannot regenerate.

| File | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| `reaper.db` | Your policies, decisions, whitelist and audit trail. |
| `secret.key` | The master key for every stored credential. |
| `secret.salt` | Minted per install, and paired with that key. Both are needed to decrypt. |

<Callout tone="caution">
  **`secret.key` on its own is not enough.** The key is stretched with `secret.salt` before
  anything is decrypted, so a backup carrying one without the other reads exactly like a backup
  carrying neither. Take all three files, every time.
</Callout>

## Let Reaper do it

Settings, Backup produces one archive with all three in it, which removes the chance of taking
two files out of three by hand.

Restoring that archive asks for your admin password. Downloading it asks only that you are
signed in, so treat the file the way you would treat the database.

<Callout tone="caution">
  **Treat the archive and `reaper.db` as sensitive.** They hold the credentials for every
  service Reaper talks to, including a Plex token that grants full control of your Plex account.
  Encryption at rest guards the file on disk. Keep it out of public issue reports and support
  threads all the same.
</Callout>

## The one you can throw away

`cache.db` sits next to them and is disposable by design. It is other people's data mirrored
locally, meaning watch history and the IMDb dataset, and Reaper rebuilds it.

Separating them states the invariant out loud: nothing in `cache.db` is a source of truth.

## Restoring

Put all three files back in the data volume and start the container. Migrations run before the
app accepts a connection, so a database from an older version is brought forward on the way up.

Restore `reaper.db` without its matching key and salt and every stored credential is unreadable
and must be entered again. The policies, decisions and audit trail survive.

<Callout tone="caution">
  **Re-entering a credential against the wrong key destroys the good one.** If services come
  back unreadable, put the right key and salt in place first. Saving over an unreadable
  credential writes new ciphertext under the wrong key, and the original is then gone.
</Callout>
